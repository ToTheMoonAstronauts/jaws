Can chewing gum sharpen your focus?

JAWZR team · 4 November 2026 · 4 min read

Chewing gum before an exam is an old tip. There is some research behind it, but the effect is smaller and shorter than the tip suggests.

In shortIn a 2011 study, students who chewed gum for five minutes before a test did better on several tasks, but only for the first 15–20 minutes. Chewing throughout the test gave no advantage. Researchers still debate the size of the effect.

The study

Researchers at St. Lawrence University in New York gave students a set of cognitive tests. Some chewed gum for five minutes before starting, some chewed during the tests and some didn't chew at all.1

Those who chewed beforehand performed better on several of the tasks. But the advantage only lasted for roughly the first 15 to 20 minutes. Students who chewed all the way through the tests did no better than those who didn't chew.

Why it might work

The authors' explanation is "mastication-induced arousal": chewing briefly raises alertness, a bit like a short warm-up for the brain. Chewing during a task may cancel that out because your brain then has to share attention between chewing and thinking.

What it doesn't mean

  • It is a short-term alertness effect, not a lasting boost to intelligence or memory.
  • Studies on gum and cognition give mixed results. Effects on alertness show up more consistently than effects on memory.
  • Most studies used ordinary gum, not hard training gum.

How to use it

If you want to try it, time your session: a JAWZR round before deep work or a workout, then put the gum away while you focus. It's a handy way to fit your daily training into the day, with a possible small bonus.
