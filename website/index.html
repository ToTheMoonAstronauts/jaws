Fits the routine you already have

Ten minutes, anywhere

No device to clean, nothing to explain to the person next to you. A session runs at your desk, on the commute or between sets.

Train it like any other muscle

Load. Rest. Repeat.

Short daily sessions beat chewing all afternoon. Alternate full and light days and never go past 15 minutes a day.

The session

One block, two or three rounds

Treat it like a set at the gym: controlled reps, a rest, then repeat. More chewing is not more progress: alternate a full day with a light day.

  1. Warm it up~1 min

    Rest the piece on your back molars. Let it soften slightly in your mouth, then bite down gradually. A cold, hard bite can crack the piece.

  2. Slow, heavy chews3 min

    Controlled tempo, pressure building on every bite. Keep it on the molars, never the front teeth.

  3. Faster chews2 min

    Pick up the pace without losing control. Switch sides evenly.

  4. Rest3 min

    Jaw relaxed, lips closed, breathe through your nose.

  5. Repeat and stop2–3 rounds

    Stay within 10–15 minutes of active chewing, then throw the piece away. Use a fresh one next session.

  6. Tomorrow: light day

    One round, about 8 minutes. Alternate full and light days for 30 days. If your jaw clicks, aches or feels tight the next morning, take a day off.

Results, honestly

What jaw training can change

A defined jawline is bone, body fat, skin and muscle. Training only touches the muscle. We'd rather you know that before you buy than after.

Trainable

Bite strength

Regular chewing training raises bite force. A 6-month randomised trial at Yonsei University saw it rise within three months.

Biggest lever

Body fat

Lower body fat is what reveals the angle of your jaw. Training the masseter adds width at the back of the jaw, and it only shows when you're lean.

Not trainable

Bone shape

No gum reshapes your jawbone or removes fat under the chin. Anyone promising a new face in two weeks is selling a photo.

Jung et al. 202458 adults, gum chewing 3×/day for 6 months: bite force up; masseter thickness and jaw shape unchanged in that protocol (regular, not ultra-hard, gum).
Park et al. 2020Older adults, chewing-exercise device 20 min/day, 5 days/week for 6 weeks: bite force and masseter thickness both increased.
Dental viewDentists warn that very long or daily hard chewing can strain the jaw joint and damage fillings. That's why our plan caps you at 15 minutes a day and alternates full and light days.

Safety

Who shouldn't chew it

Under 14s. The gum is a training tool, not candy.
Anyone with jaw-joint (TMJ) pain, clicking or locking. Ask a dentist first.
Loose fillings, crowns, veneers, implants or recent dental work. Skip Ultra Hard entirely.
Braces or aligners. Wait until treatment is finished.
Stop at sharp pain. Normal is a muscle burn that fades within a day.
Never swallow the piece. Keep away from small children.

FAQ

Questions

Hard or Ultra Hard: where do I start?

Start with Hard. It has more flavour and a range of motion that lets you learn the technique. Move up once a full session feels easy. Ultra Hard is the denser piece with very little flavour; some people chew one of each together.

When will I notice anything?

The jaw "pump" and burn come in the first session. Strength builds over weeks. Any visible change depends mostly on your body fat, so pair training with diet and exercise and judge progress over months, not days.

Why does the flavour fade so quickly?

Flavour oils and softeners make gum softer. To keep the piece hard, flavour sits mostly in the outer coating. Hard keeps flavour longer than Ultra Hard.

How long does one pack last?

Depends on the pieces per pack: the 30-day plan uses one fresh piece a day. pieces per pack

Will my face get wider?

A bigger masseter adds width at the back corner of the jaw. If you want a slimmer lower face, this isn't the product for you.
